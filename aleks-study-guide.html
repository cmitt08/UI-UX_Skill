1-Hour Crash Guide

ALEKS Math Placement — Hit 61+

The smartest path to 61 isn't knowing hard math — it's being near-perfect on the easy-to-medium math. This guide shows you exactly why, what to review right now, and how to play the test itself.

01 The Strategy (read this first — 5 min)

How ALEKS actually works

  • Your score = the % of ~300 topics ALEKS believes you've mastered. A 61 means "mastered 61% of the topic pool" — it is not "61% of questions right."
  • It's adaptive: ~25–30 open-response questions (you type the answer — no multiple choice, no guessing your way through).
  • Every question is a probe. Get it right → ALEKS credits you with that topic and the easier topics that lead up to it. Get it wrong → ALEKS assumes you're missing that whole cluster and drops down.
  • There's generally no penalty for taking your time — most schools allow 2–3 hours. Nobody runs out of time; people lose points to careless errors.

Why 61 is very reachable

The topic pool is roughly: arithmetic & percents, linear equations & graphs, systems, exponents & polynomials, factoring & quadratics, rational and radical expressions, functions, geometry — and then a top layer of logs, exponentials, and trig.

Everything below trig/logs is about two-thirds of the pool. So the winning move is:

  1. Be flawless on arithmetic → intermediate algebra. These are "easy" topics, but a careless miss here is expensive — one wrong answer can wipe out credit for a whole cluster of topics you actually know.
  2. Fight for every algebra question. Factoring, quadratic formula, exponent rules, function evaluation — these are the swing votes that carry you from the 50s into the 60s.
  3. Don't sweat trig/logs. Attempt them if they look familiar, but a miss up there barely matters for a 61.

Safety net: you almost certainly get retakes

Most schools give 4–5 attempts, keep your highest score, and unlock a personalized Prep and Learning Module after the first try (studying ~3–6 hours in it raises placement by about one course level on average). So today's attempt is low-stakes: play it seriously, but know that a miss today is a diagnostic, not a disaster. Check your school's retake policy.

03 The must-know review

MUST nail these cold   SHOULD big score movers   BONUS only if time allows

MUST Fractions, decimals & signed numbers

  • Add/subtract fractions: get a common denominator first. a/b + c/d = (ad + bc)/bd
  • Multiply: straight across. Divide: flip the second and multiply. (a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (a/b)·(d/c)
  • Always reduce your final answer — ALEKS often requires simplest form.
  • Signs: negative × negative = positive; subtracting a negative = adding. 5 − (−3) = 8
  • Order of operations: Parentheses → Exponents → Multiply/Divide (left to right) → Add/Subtract (left to right).
Try: 2/3 + 3/4  →  8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12  |  −3² = −9 but (−3)² = 9 (a classic ALEKS trap)

MUST Percents, ratios & proportions

  • "x% of N" → (x/100) · N.   Percent change: (new − old)/old × 100
  • Proportions: cross-multiply. a/b = c/d ⟹ ad = bc
  • Word-problem translation: "is" = equals, "of" = multiply, "what" = x.
Try: A $80 jacket is discounted 35%. Price? → 80 × 0.65 = $52
Try: 18 is what percent of 45? → 18/45 = 0.40 = 40%

MUST Solving linear equations & inequalities

  • Undo in reverse order; whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Distribute first, collect x's on one side.
  • Equations with fractions: multiply every term by the LCD to clear them immediately.
  • Inequalities: same rules, but flip the sign when multiplying/dividing by a negative.
  • Literal equations ("solve for w"): treat every other letter like a number.
Try: 3(x − 4) = 2x + 5 → 3x − 12 = 2x + 5 → x = 17
Try: −2x > 10 → x < −5 (sign flipped!)

MUST Lines, slope & graphing

  • Slope through two points: m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)
  • Slope-intercept: y = mx + b (m = slope, b = y-intercept). Point-slope: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)
  • Parallel lines: same slope. Perpendicular: negative reciprocal (m and −1/m).
  • Intercepts: x-intercept → set y = 0; y-intercept → set x = 0.
Try: Line through (1, 2) and (3, 8): m = (8−2)/(3−1) = 3 → y − 2 = 3(x − 1) → y = 3x − 1

SHOULD Systems of equations

  • Substitution: solve one equation for a variable, plug into the other.
  • Elimination: scale one/both equations so a variable cancels when you add them.
Try: x + y = 10, x − y = 4 → add: 2x = 14 → x = 7, y = 3

SHOULD Exponent rules & polynomials

  • xᵃ·xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ   xᵃ/xᵇ = xᵃ⁻ᵇ   (xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃᵇ   x⁰ = 1   x⁻ⁿ = 1/xⁿ
  • FOIL: (a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd. Memorize (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b² — it is not a² + b².
  • Combine like terms only (3x² + 5x can't be combined).
Try: (2x³y)² · x = 4x⁶y² · x = 4x⁷y²  |  (x + 5)(x − 3) = x² + 2x − 15

SHOULD Factoring & quadratic equations — the biggest score movers

  • Always pull out the GCF first: 6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3)
  • Trinomials x² + bx + c: find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.
  • Difference of squares: a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b)
  • To solve: set = 0, factor, set each factor to 0. If it won't factor, quadratic formula:
    x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a)
  • Vertex of y = ax² + bx + c is at x = −b/(2a).
Try: x² − 5x − 14 = 0 → (x − 7)(x + 2) = 0 → x = 7 or x = −2

SHOULD Radicals & rational expressions

  • Simplify roots by pulling out perfect squares: √72 = √36·√2 = 6√2
  • √a·√b = √(ab), but √(a+b) ≠ √a + √b (trap!)
  • Fractional exponents: x^(1/2) = √x, x^(3/2) = (√x)³
  • Rational expressions: factor top and bottom, cancel whole factors only — never cancel terms across a + sign.
Try: (x² − 9)/(x + 3) = (x+3)(x−3)/(x+3) = x − 3

SHOULD Functions

  • f(x) is just "plug it in": if f(x) = x² − 3x, then f(−2) = 4 + 6 = 10. Use parentheses when substituting negatives.
  • Domain: exclude values that make a denominator 0 or a square root negative.
  • Composition: (f ∘ g)(x) = f(g(x)) — work inside-out.
Try: f(x) = 2x + 1, g(x) = x². f(g(3)) = f(9) = 19

BONUS Only if you have spare time (not needed for 61)

  • Logs: log_b(x) = y ⟺ bʸ = x, so log₂ 8 = 3.   log(ab) = log a + log b.
  • Right-triangle trig: SOH-CAH-TOA — sin = opp/hyp, cos = adj/hyp, tan = opp/adj.
  • Exponential growth: A = P(1 + r)ᵗ

If a trig/log question appears and looks alien, it means you're doing well — the test climbed above your target. Attempt it if you can; if it's truly foreign, "I haven't learned this yet" is acceptable up here only.

04 12-problem warm-up (do on paper)

  1. Compute: 5/6 − 3/8
  2. Compute: 4 − 2(3 − 7)² ÷ 8
  3. A phone costs $120 after a 25% discount. What was the original price?
  4. Solve: 2(3x − 1) = 4x + 10
  5. Solve: (x/3) + (x/4) = 7
  6. Solve for h: V = ⅓·B·h
  7. Find the equation of the line through (−2, 5) with slope −3.
  8. Solve the system: 2x + y = 7 and x − y = 2
  9. Simplify: (3x²y⁻¹)² · y³
  10. Factor completely: 2x² − 8x − 42
  11. Solve: x² = 5x + 6
  12. If f(x) = x² − 4x + 1, find f(−3).
Show answers
  1. 11/24 (LCD 24: 20/24 − 9/24)
  2. 0 (4 − 2(16)÷8 = 4 − 32÷8 = 4 − 4)
  3. $160 (120 = 0.75 × original)
  4. x = 6 (6x − 2 = 4x + 10)
  5. x = 12 (multiply by 12: 4x + 3x = 84)
  6. h = 3V/B
  7. y = −3x − 1 (y − 5 = −3(x + 2))
  8. x = 3, y = 1 (add the equations: 3x = 9)
  9. 9x⁴y (9x⁴y⁻² · y³)
  10. 2(x − 7)(x + 3) (GCF 2 first)
  11. x = 6 or x = −1 (x² − 5x − 6 = 0 → (x−6)(x+1))
  12. 22 (9 + 12 + 1 — parentheses around −3!)

Scoring yourself: 10+ right → you're in great shape for 61. 7–9 → re-skim the sections you missed; those are your money topics. Under 7 → focus your remaining minutes entirely on problems 1–7 (the MUST tier) — perfect fundamentals alone put you in the 50s–60s, and the retake with ALEKS's learning module will finish the job.

05 Test-day tactics

Do

  • Work every problem on paper first, then type the answer. Mental math is where 61s die.
  • Go slow. You likely have 2–3 hours for ~30 questions — that's 4+ minutes each. Use them.
  • Double-check before submitting — you cannot go back. Re-read the question: did it ask for x, or for x + 2? Simplified fraction? Units?
  • Take the ALEKS answer-input tutorial seriously — learn the palette for fractions, exponents, and roots so you never lose a point to formatting.
  • Use the on-screen ALEKS calculator when it appears — if the button shows up, the problem expects it. (No personal calculators.)
  • Plug your answer back in when solving equations — a 10-second check that catches most errors.
  • Attempt everything that looks even vaguely familiar. Partial knowledge often produces right answers, and right answers credit whole topic clusters.

Don't

  • Don't click "I haven't learned this yet" on anything you might be able to do — it tells ALEKS to zero out that topic and its neighbors. Reserve it for genuinely never-seen material (weird trig/log stuff).
  • Don't rush the early questions. The first stretch calibrates the whole test — careless misses early cost the most.
  • Don't use outside help, phones, or websites. It's a placement tool — an inflated score puts you in a course you'll drown in (and proctored versions will catch it).
  • Don't panic when questions get hard. Hard questions mean you're winning — the test climbs until you miss. Everyone ends around 50% right; that's the design.
  • Don't leave an answer half-simplified: reduce fractions, simplify radicals, combine like terms.
  • Don't cram new material in the last 10 minutes. Accuracy on what you know beats a shaky grasp of what you don't.

The one-sentence version

Slow down, show work on paper, be perfect on the algebra you already know, never surrender a question you can attempt — and remember that hard questions mean it's going well. Good luck — you've got this. 🎯
